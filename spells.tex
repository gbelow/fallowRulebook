\section{Sorcery basics}

Sorcery is the ability to affect things outside of self using only the mind. Sorcery tests can be used on any magical artifact that can be affected. 

Spells are just useful abilities that a character can train and improve at for repeated usage. They do not represent everything that can be done with sorcery.

\subsection{Learning spells}

Spells can be learned in three different ways: intuitively, as a wizard and as a cleric.

\textbf{Intuitively:} A specific spell can be learned through simple trial and error under the right circumstances. Doing it this way costs 6 XP, and the talent used to learn is the Sorcery proficiency level or devotion (if the spell is in the divinities list). It is not possible to learn spells with knowledge requirements above 3 this way.

The skill to perform spells this way is initially 0. The spell can be practiced for 1 XP to increase it to 1 and then again by 2XP to increase it to 2 and so on increasing the cost by 1 to increase it further by 1 point. 

In case a spell is learnt through the wizard or cleric method, it is possible to improve on a specific spell starting at the skill level that the character has when they learn it. Always match the skill of an intuitive spell to the skill of the other methods, if they are higher.

\textbf{Wizard:} A wizard is a scientist of magic. They learn their spells through books and experimentation. The knowledges related to magic are Alchemy, Animancy, Biomancy and Shamanism. The \textbf{Knowledge level + Sorcery} is used as a skill to perform spells.

Wizards can attempt to interact with anything that has magical properties, without prior learning. This works like a regular sorcery test, but must be done in a risky manner. For every knowledge point missing compared to the object's requirement, increase difficulty by 5.

The wizard can learn spells from grimoires or through experimentation within an exploration turn, given that the required materials are present. Make a sorcery test and learn on a hit or crit. It has its difficulty increased by 5 for every knowledge point missing. After the spell is learned, there is no knowledge penalty to perform it.

\textbf{Cleric:} can cast every spell in the deity's list, as long as it has the required devotion level. The \textbf{Devotion level + Sorcery} is used as a skill to perform divine spells.

Clerics can also attempt to cast miracles. When they do that, they use \textbf{2x Devotion} as a skill, and can try to cast any spell, regardless of devotion level, but get a -3 penalty on the test per devotion level under the spell's knowledge requirement and must perform them in a risky manner. It is impossible to cast spells this way in places outside the influence area of the deity. In a temple or holy place, the effect is opposite, and the difficulty to cast is reduced. 

\subsection{Casting spells}

%TN
Casting a spell is a skill test against a spell DL. Any points above a hit against the DL are converted into SOPs, which can be used to improve spells, as explained in the spell modifications. 

Casting a spell costs AP and STA during combat or exhaustion during exploration. To cast any spell, make a sorcery test against the \textbf{Spell Casting DL} to determine if the spell was successfully cast or not. In case of miss, it fails and the AP and STA are lost. 

Spells require using a focus surge to be cast in combat scenes.

\textbf{Concentration:} All sorcery tests require concentration. No other action or reaction can be performed while concentrating. Being interrupted causes the loss of concentration. Sometimes, a character's AP may not be enough to cover the action's requirements. In that case, they can maintain concentration and continue acting in the next turn. Losing concentration causes all AP and STA spent to be lost. 



\textbf{Delayed Spells:} Any spell can be delayed, meaning that the casting can be completed, but the release is postponed. The character must concentrate on the spell until the release, which can be triggered by a previously prepared action. If a trigger does not occur, remake the sorcery test every turn to keep the spell ready.

\textbf{Requirements:} Some spells may even be impossible to cast if the minimum conditions are not met. Making it rain in the desert is impossible if there is no water in the sky.

\textbf{Stealth and Sorcery:} Spells are imperceptible through normal senses before they cause their effect. Once their effect occurs, they can be identified with a test of the skill used to cast the spell.

\textbf{Relationship between Size and Sorcery:} Any spell that has DM, SM, RM or VM marked in it is scaled to character size.


\subsection{Types of Spells and Modifications} %---------- TYPES

Enhancements are modifications that casters can make to their spells to make them more powerful or better suited to their needs. Each of them have a cost in SOPs to be used.

\textbf{Quicken Spell:} Increases spell DL by 4 to allow it to be cast during any surge and not cause oportunity attacks. \\

\textbf{Extend Spell:} Spend 3 SOPs to increase the casting range of a spell by +100\%, then +200\%, +300\%, and so on in progression.\\

\textbf{Enhance Spell:} Spend 4 SOPs to improve spells in a special way described in the spell.  \\

\textbf{Amplify Spell:} Spend 4 SOPs to amplify spells. It can be used when the spell has its effects multiplied by DM, SM, RM, and VM. The number of times it can be increased depends on environmental conditions and the material used. The amount of material or charges consumed is multiplied by the VM. \\

% \textbf{Extend Charge Duration:} Spend 4 SOPs to increase the charge duration of charged spells by +100\%, then +200\%, +300\%, and so on in progression. \\

\textbf{Effortless Spell:} Spend 6 SOPs to rest while casting a spell. The spell must spend the highest between the rest's AP cost and the spell's AP cost. If used outside combat, reduces the exhaustion cost of a spell by half, rounded down.  \\

The types of spells are:

\begin{proplist}
    \item \textbf{Instant:} Happens as the casting is complete.
    \item \textbf{Sustained Spells:} are actions that can be performed continuously while concentration is maintained. Their effect ends when the caster stops concentrating on it. The cost must be paid at the beginning of the next round to maintain the spell effect.
    \item \textbf{Charged:} activates an object that stays charged. The charge can be released when a trigger is activated if there is one. The charge lasts until the end of the combat for spells that cost AP or a number of ETs, for those that last ETs.
    \item \textbf{Curse:} are spells that stay active until the target has hit or crit on a test to shrug it off. Tests to resist curses are made automatically every start of round or start of ET.
\end{proplist}

\section{Spell Design Principles:}

\begin{proplist}
    \item \textbf{No turn durations:} Don't make spell durations in turns. Turn based durations add bookkeeping during combat. Make dispell triggers instead, like will test or insight test.
    \item \textbf{Survival spell durations:} Durations should be in ETs.
    \item \textbf{No summoning:} Matter cannot be created. Spirits are incorporeal.
    \item \textbf{No free energy:} the physical energy must come from the target of the spell. The spell merely activates the event telepathically when it otherwise would not.
    \item \textbf{No magical buffs or debuffs in combat:} Buffs and debuffs must not give bonuses to skills or attributes directly. Those are hard to remember and add bookkeeping.
    \item \textbf{Ranges:} Keep it under 30m. Charging spells should be touch. Projectiles can shoot further.
    \item \textbf{Area of effect:} Keep explosions between 2m - 5m radius. Environmentals can be of any size.
    \item \textbf{Spell scaling:} Scaling is meant to be done by means of spell enhancement. No spells should be a bigger version of another.
\end{proplist}

\textbf{Exception:} Shamanism spells can break these principles. They work by allying with local entities, which have different limitations.

\section{Shamanism}

The ability to interpret, negotiate and channel the power of the spiritual forces of the world. This kind of magic is made by communing with spirits, learning about their motivations and convincing them to help somehow.

Spirits make many demands in order to help or lend their abilities. Types of demand: behavioral, offering, task. 

\begin{itemize}
    \item Behaviorial: can be avoid eating meat, wearing something, being honest, etc. Faulty behavior will cease the concession of power. It may take a while for a spirit to be convinced to lend its power.
    \item Offering: can be an item to be placed somewhere, something to be burned, blood to be given. This works like an exchange: give something and get something in return.
    \item Task: can be to defeat someone or drive away something, go somewhere and plant seeds, or any other mission. A task may have a deadline or a dealbreaker that will break the contract.
\end{itemize}

Spirits can be found through synesthesia or through attempts to interact with magical objects. Some spirits can speak, others cannot. Discovering a spirit's intentions can be half the challenge. 

% Spirits can have their power resonate through special items.

There are 5 types of entity that can be negotiated with:

\textbf{ Incorporeal entities:} Shadows, Echoes, wraiths. These have skills and knowledge that they can share.

\textbf{ Sentient Objects:} work like gatekeepers for magical object, where negotiation is necessary for usage. 

\textbf{ Elementals: } elements of the world that respond to magic. They can be manipulated using alchemy when convinced to help. 

\textbf{ Divinities: } the gods of the world. They respond to clerics and use divination as the skill to use their abilities. 

\textbf{ Life forms: } some living beings, like trees, fungi or bacteria may have a spiritual presence that allows bargaining with. Once the bargain is struck, use Biomancy as the skill to control use the spirit's abilities.
