\section{Weapons Properties}

A weapon is a tool used in combat to enhance the character's abilities. Each weapon or shield has at least one form of attack. Each form has its own properties: \\

All weapon attacks are made out of metal, unless otherwise stated.

\noindent
\begin{proplist}
\item \textbf{Grapple:} This attack is used for grappling actions.
\item \textbf{Sweeping:} Allows for Sweeping attacks.
\item \textbf{Braced:} This attack can be a braced attack.
\item \textbf{Shaft:} This attack represents the shaft of the weapon.
\item \textbf{Short, Long I/II:} Short-range attacks can be used in grappling combat but have a range of 1m x RM. Long-range attacks have an extended range of 1m x RM per level and cannot be used in grappling combat.  
\item \textbf{DEF:} Attacks that have DEF can be used to block. The blocking value is equal to STR if weapon is one handed, and 2x STR if two handed or a shield. This value scales with weapon size.
\item \textbf{STR x:} Strength requirement. Cannot use quick shot unless STR is +3 points higher than the requirement.
\item \textbf{Hook:} This attack can be used for hooked attacks.
\item \textbf{One/Two hands:} One-handed attacks use only one hand. Two-handed weapons require both hands. 
\item \textbf{Heavy I/II/III:} Allows heavy attacks. Having a higher degree of heavy allows using any lower degree. Having heavy I-III or similar means that heavy I is minimum, and normal attacks are not allowed.
\item \textbf{Piercing:} Grazes behave like a miss. Deal half the amount of IL damage per tier to body and limb and cannot amputate. Only breaks objects when damage > 2x RES.
\item \textbf{Bladed:} allows using the extra cut additional effect and can be sharpened.
\item \textbf{Penetrating:} allows using the penetrating additional effect. Can only be used in piercing attacks.
\item \textbf{Precise:} allows performing armor bypass additional effect.
\item \textbf{Vicious:} causes double bleeding.
\item \textbf{RES:} indicates RES of that part of the weapon, which is the threshold for gear breakage.
\item \textbf{Slow, Fast, UF:} Property only for ranged weapons. Slow: gets -2 accuracy when the opponent defends actively. Fast: can only be blocked with a shield. Ultra fast(UF): like fast, but it is automatically penetrating. 
\item \textbf{Draw:} Attack can be performed at the same time as drawing the weapon, and drawing can be done with any action surge.
\item \textbf{Reload:} The weapon requires reloading to attack again.
\end{proplist}

\textbf{Scaling weapons:} The weapons in the tables are human-sized and serve as a basis for size scaling. When scaling weapon size, multiply damages and RES by the DM and reach by the RM.

\textbf{Size Scaling:} Every piece of gear in the list is made for a size 3 creature. Wielding a weapon that is larger than appropriate increases the cost of AP for all attacks by +1, and uses STR-5 in the contribution of STR to damage. This penalty applies to any contribution of STR for any attack, including braced, hooked, and heavy. Wielding a weapon 2 size categories larger is impossible.

\end{multicols}

\section{ Weapons}

\subsection{Melee Weapons}

\textbf{ Unarmed:} Corresponds to the hands of a humanoid character. It has punch and grapple as attacks. The punch deals damage based on the character's strength. A punch is unlikely to damage another character wearing armor, but it can stun when hitting the head. A heavy attack that overcomes armor coverage may be enough to knock someone out. The grapple does not deal damage, but serves both to hold the opponent and perform grappling maneuvers as well as to block some damage.

\textbf{Brass Knuckles:} are a stronger version of the fists that are part of the armor. The character receives penalties for prestidigitation, accuracy, and climbing, but gains more coverage in the armor and has a much stronger and more resilient punch.

\textbf{Rondel:} is a dagger focused on destroying armored opponents. Its penetration is quite high, making opponents wearing a chain shirt or chainmail extremely vulnerable. Being a precise with short range, the rondel is ideal for grappling combat. Even when its penetration is insufficient against armor, the rondel, being a precise weapon, can devastate the opponent if it lands a vulnerable attack.

\textbf{Dagger:} are extremely versatile weapons. They can be used as tools or as weapons and are easily concealed. Daggers are also used as defensive tools, making them useful when held in the non-dominant hand.

\textbf{Whip:} is the only light weapon with long range. Its damage and penetration are insufficient to injure any armored opponent, but its great advantage lies in the grapple attack. Keeping a target under control at a distance combined with the ability to drag it towards you can be a great strategic move against agile opponents, leaving them open to braced and opportunity attacks, as well as reducing their ability to defend. There are modified versions of whips, such as the electric whip, which allows electrical abilities to hit grappled opponents.

\textbf{Short Sword:} It is a larger version of a dagger, suitable for public use without drawing attention and easy to draw. Although it is too large to be ideal for grappling combat, it has stronger attacks and offers better defensive potential. It is a good secondary weapon or for urban and enclosed environments.

\textbf{Rapier:} It is the fastest one-handed long weapon available. They are excellent for duels against lightly armored opponents, as their flurries of strikes are very difficult to defend against, and their range provides a significant advantage over shorter weapons. Rapiers are commonly seen in urban settings and are easily carried in enclosed spaces. They can also be thrown, making them a very versatile weapon. Short spears are very cheap and common, but they are unusual in an urban environment and should be carried in one hand or strapped to the back. Due to being a wooden shaft weapon, the durability of spears can become an issue when used against highly rigid objects or when used to block cutting weapons.

\textbf{Mace:} It is a weapon focused on taking down armored opponents. Its best feature is its heavy strike, which is relatively quick compared to the heavy strikes of other weapons. The durability of the mace is very high when used offensively, but its shaft makes it fragile defensively. Some maces can be made entirely of metal.

\textbf{Battle Axe:} It is a very good option against wooden objects or opponents with low-rigidity armor. Its blade combined with its weight makes it an excellent weapon for destroying shields. The axe is slower than swords, and its attacks can leave the user open to enemy offensives, so it is wise to make each attack count. Its defensive potential is low, as it is a shaft weapon. It is better to use the axe alongside a shield to compensate. The battle axe has a spike on the back, which is a better option against opponents whose armor the blade cannot penetrate.

\subsection{Ranged Weapons}

\textbf{Net:} it is a good weapon for ambushes or hunts. When thrown against groups of opponents, it can provide a decisive advantage by entangling several of them. Its preparation time is very long, making it impractical to throw multiple times in the same combat.

\textbf{Grappling Hook and Rope:} it is a weapon that, when it hits, allows you to pull a target. It is also a weapon that requires reload time, making multiple shots difficult. The grappling hook and rope can also be used as a climbing tool.

\textbf{Grenade:} a throwable explosive ignited by a mundane fuse or impact. \magic The grenade can also be magically activated in different ways to produce various effects.

\textbf{Short Bow:} it is the lightest bow. The strength required to draw it is low, allowing for faster shooting techniques. Short bows have a shorter range and deal less damage than other bows, making them poor choices against resilient opponents. Short bows can use broadhead arrows for more damage or bodkin arrows for better penetration.

\textbf{Long Bow:} it is the most common bow on the battlefield. It has enough power to penetrate medium armor when it hits directly. Drawing a bow like this requires above-average muscle strength.

\textbf{Heavy Bow:} this type of bow is so heavy that only the heaviest armors can stop its arrows. The strength required to draw such a bow is so great that very few humanoids are capable of using it. This weapon can use larger arrows, intended to take down large animals and heavily armored soldiers.

\textbf{Crossbow:} the crossbow is a weapon that can be fired quickly but requires reload time. The power of a crossbow shot is equivalent to that of a long bow.

\textbf{Arbalest:} the arbalest is an oversized crossbow that needs a lever-based reloading system to be drawn by an average person. The power of its shots is comparable to that of a heavy bow.

\section{Armors}

Armors have prot, which is used as damage reduction against impacts. RES is both the armor breakage threshold and the damage reduction against CUT, since the armor must be destroyed to allow cutting damage to hit the wearer.

\textbf{Burden penalties:} The penalties shown in the armor and shield tables derive from the additional weight of the armor and are reflected as a penalty in the AGI and STA attribute values. The penalties from multiple gear stack. Burden penalties are reduced or increased based on STR, where burden penalty is modified by $(STR-10)/3$

\textbf{Fiber armors} require the user to defend against most attacks actively, but they prevent the damage from being severe if hit. Cloth armors have very few penalties and a hardness of 2.

\textbf{Metallic armors} cause the character to sink 1m per turn while swimming, on top of whatever they get in their swimming test. Metal armors cannot be cut without the penetrating property.

\textbf{Rigid armors:} receive and deal damage from weapons that hit them, thus having a RES value. Their protection against impact is greater, as the armor absorbs a larger portion.

\textbf{Gauntlets:} Gauntlets give -3 to prestidigitation, -3 to accuracy and climbing, but the armor protects the vulnerability of the hands. A pair of gauntlets costs 15 GP or 15\% of the armor's value.

\textbf{Closed helmet:} gives -2 to reflexes, -2 to detection, and makes swimming always suffocating, but armor bypass is impossible in the head. Opening and closing the visor of a closed helmet is a standard action. A closed helmet costs 15 GP or 15\% of the armor's value.

\textbf{Gauntlets and closed helmet:} It is possible to remove the gloves and closed helmets, but removing each of these eliminates their bonuses.

\textbf{Donning and Doffing armor:} Costs a standard action to don/doff helmet, gauntlets and small armor. Costs 6 AP to don/doff medium armors and several minutes to don/doff large ones. It is possible to doff a large armor by cutting it with a blade and spending 2 full turns, or 16 AP on it. This leaves the armor damaged.

\textbf{Armor layers:} For any armor marked with two values for RES, like 10/5, the second and lower value is the inner layer, which is made of the same material as the outer one by default. Unlayered armor cannot be bypassed.

\textbf{Common clothing:} is made of cotton or linen. It protects against very light injuries, such as bumping a shoulder against a rock or being scratched by a cat. Common people wear clothes of this type.

\textbf{Padded Armor:} is a garment with many layers made of cotton or linen, accompanied by boots and a metal helmet. It is very thick, which makes it heavy and reduces the user's mobility. This armor surprisingly offers good protection, but it is not made of a material designed to withstand many cuts. A high-quality padded armor would be much more expensive but would improve resistance to cuts.

\textbf{Greatcoat:} is made from materials designed to protect the user in battle. It can be made of special leather or some durable plant fiber. Its great advantage is the mobility it provides, combined with reasonable protection against medium piercing weapons. If the user is hit directly by blades, the material will give way, but in the case of grazes, the damage should be completely mitigated. There may be variations of the armor with greater rigidity or less weight.

\textbf{Chain Shirt: } is the only metallic armor among the light ones. Its value is somewhat higher than the others because the difficulty of production is greater. The chain shirt is worn over a greatcoat.

\textbf{Hauberk:} the first and lightest of the medium armors. It is made of thicker rings than the chain shirt and provides better coverage for the legs and neck. As it is still a fairly flexible armor, it allows heavy impacts to injure its user, but it also offers few penalties to mobility.

\textbf{Brigandine:} is made of rectangular metal plates sewn inside a fabric vest. Since the metal plates can slide over each other, the cost to mobility is not so high. It can be complemented with a closed helmet and gauntlets during a major battle, but it can also be used without the gauntlets to allow free use of ranged combat weapons. Most professional soldiers use this type of armor in their daily lives, as it has a high cost but is not prohibitive, and it is also not so heavy that activities like climbing hills or trees become impossible.

\textbf{Half Armor: } is a lighter version of full armor. Several of the more restrictive pieces are removed so that the armor remains medium. As it retains the steel breastplate, it is very good against projectiles. Only those who find a gap in the armor will be able to injure the wearer. This type of armor is often chosen by archers in open field battles.

\textbf{Full Armor: } is the traditional armor of the medieval knight. Those who wear such armor want to survive a rain of steel or expend all their energy in attacking while letting the armor defend them. It comes standard with gauntlets and a closed helmet, providing superior coverage and preventing almost any attack from hitting a vulnerable point. Its thick steel plates offer high resistance against all types of damage, and its rigidity is so high that it is common for more fragile weapons, like swords, to break upon impact. Adventuring in this armor is extremely tiring. There is an even more exaggerated version of this armor, which is the reinforced one.

\section{Smithing and maintenance}

\subsection{Equipment Breakage}

The equipment breakage function is optional.

\textbf{Equipment Breakage:} Objects can be destroyed by any kind of damage. The object's RES represents their resistance to both cutting and blunt damage. Destroying an object by burning is a special case that depends on the material. The order of magnitude of burning damage is much higher than what would be lethal to a creature, so it is not necessary to have a specific value for it.

Objects can deal and take damage whenever they collide with something else, whether the collision is caused by themselves or by the other object. Cutting damage is dealt exclusively by the attacking object to its target, while impact is dealt to both.

\textbf{Breakage throuh physical damage:} compare blunt and cutting damage from the attacking object to the RES of the receiving one. Whenever a piece of equipment can be cut and receives cutting or blunt damage >= RES, the equipment has a 1 in 6 chance to break. If it receives both >= RES, it has 50\% chance. If any single damage exceeds RES twice, the item breaks instantly.

\textbf{Breakage and Hardness:} Collisions involving materials of hardness 1 and 2 do not pose a risk of breakage to other materials.

% \textbf{Breakage through fire/radiant:} 

\textbf{Weapon Breakage:} A broken weapon can no longer be used and costs 50\% of its original value to be repaired. Weapons that have a shaft require the replacement of the shaft.

\textbf{Armor Breakage:} When armor breaks, it is considered pitted. Repairing armor costs 20\% of the armor's value.

\subsection{Equipment Repair}

\textbf{Sharpening:} Is an action that takes one exploration turn and applies the sharp condition. Requires a sharpening stone. This does not work on precise weapons, because they are already considered sharp.

\textbf{Repairing gear:} Requires an armor rapair kit. Make a smithing test against armor DL. Hits and crits fix in 1 exploration turn. grazes take 2 and misses just miss. Requires scraps from other armor or repair supplies.


\subsection{Characteristics and Conditions}

\textbf{Customising weapons}:  
\begin{itemize}
    \item Change base damage by up to 30\% up or down. Incresing more than that might make it a tool for every job. Double weapon value for every +10\%. 
    \item RES can be changed by up to 50\%. Double the price for every 25\%.
    \item Pointy weapons can switch between vicious and penetrating, representing a thin point for penetrating and a broader one for vicious.
    \item Penetrating+: Penetrating weapons get +1 vs deflection per improvement. Each improvement costs 10/50GP up to a maximum of +2. 
    \item Languets: Makes a shaft be made of metal. Adds +5 GP to the price.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Customising armor}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Change base damage reductions by up to 30\%. Increasing further should increase its armor penalty, for the extra weight in material. Double armor value for every +10\%. 
    \item Do not change the penalty of any armor by more than 1. 4x armor value to reduce it by 1.
    \item Higher deflection means less exploitable gaps and better deflection angles. Change it by maximum +2, and until +9 ,at most. Also increase armor penalty for the reduced mobility. Increasing it by 1 doubles armor value.
\end{itemize}

\section{Containers and Burden}

Items can be categorized as small, medium, large. 

\begin{itemize}
    \item Small items are the kind of thing that fits in a hand. Small tools, small weapons, coins, flasks and similar items are small. They tend to stay under 0.5 kg in weight and 0.5 liters in volume. 
    \item Medium items are normally cumbersome. They include one-handed weapons, books, large bottles, etc. They tend to stay under 2 kg in weight and 2 liters in volume.
    \item Large items are too big to be carried in a backpack. They are carried in hand, slung or lashed outside the backpack. These include most two-handed weapons, shields, pots, shovels. They can also be carried in vehicles and animals. 
    \item Cargo: counted in number of large items. These can only be placed in vehicles.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Hands:} Items held in hands can be of any size. 

\textbf{Containers and burden:} Travelers need to have containers to carry equipment that they are not wearing or wielding. The containers impose a level of burden to be carried, determined by their size. They do not need to have anything inside to inflict the penalty. A character can be in one of four levels of burden: light, medium, heavy, and over.

\begin{proplist}
\item \textbf{Light}: the burden has no effect.
\item \textbf{Medium}: -1 for burden penalty.
\item \textbf{Heavy}: -2 for burden penalties.
\item \textbf{Over}: -3 for burden penalties. Character is considered lame.
\end{proplist}


\textbf{Slot size and stacking:} Each container has a number of slots, which can each accomodate an item or stack of items. Slots can be small, medium or large. A small slot can hold a small item. A medium slot can hold a medium item or a stack of 5 small ones. A large slot can hold a large item, a stack of 5 medium items, or a stack of 25 small items. Only identical items can be stacked together.

\textbf{Large:} are used for large items.

\subsection{Poisons}

\textbf{Poisons} apply an effect to the target. To resist it, the victim must make a Health test. The amount of poison applied is determined by the method of application (some methods may increase the amount of poison applied).

Poisons can be delivered a through cutting injuries, ingestion, or inhalation. Each additional dose of the poison a target takes increases the Health DL by 3/2/1.

\textbf{Ingestion:} Poison must be eaten. 

\textbf{Inhalation} poisons dispersed as smoke affect everyone in the cloud at their normal DL, regardless of size.

\textbf{Cutting injuries:} Tier 1+ injuries from Cutting damage apply the full effect of the poison. Tier 0 decrease DL by 3 and higher tiers increase it by 1 per tier above 1.
