# Metaphysics — the player-facing contract

Internal. The derivations (the field theory, the orders) are deliberately absent from this file: they are design contracts for generating content, never published. This is the distilled, jargon-free contract the book makes with the reader. Each section reduces to a one-page in-world presentation (magic → `spells.tex`, monsters → `monsters.tex`).

## How magic works

Magic is not a force added to the world. It is the exploitation of a layer of physics that most creatures never perceive and no one invented.

- **Magic obeys physics.** An effect never comes from nothing; it biases what is already possible. A mage igniting a fire is striking a match, not creating flame.
- **Every working is paid for** — in fatigue, heat, disorder. Energy is never free; the cost only looks optional.
- **Magic is local.** An effect travels from a source to a target and degrades with distance and obstruction.
- **Rare routes bend distance.** Places walked, fought over, and prayed at for generations become closer than geometry says. Living use is what makes them.
- **Magic works only in predetermined ways.** It requires resonance between the caster and the materials or patterns that can carry the effect. There is no wishing; there are known forms.
- **Resonance is a learned skill.** Patterns repeat and can be classified. Knowing the theory accelerates learning — and knowing and believing are kin, which is why intellect and spirit both govern it.
- **Power scales with the caster's substrate.** A larger or finer organ sustains a larger signal, so raw force grows with the body. Psychic magic is the exception: it is an interface, and pressing the right buttons matters more than the size of the machine.
- **Any magic can be attempted as a ritual.** The mind alone suffices; ritual and bodily trappings stabilize the working. Ritual is slow magic, not different magic.
- **Mind shapes body.** Deliberate mutation is natural mutation done on purpose.
- **None of this is required to play.** A world without magic is the same physics with no one who learned to exploit it. Nothing structural breaks when it is removed.

## How gods work

- **Gods are real, and none of them made the world.** A god is a creature: the largest and most coherent mind a population can sustain.
- **Gods act through the faithful.** A devout believer can serve as conduit for a miracle — the god casting through them.
- **No god is supreme.** Any being claiming to be more than a creature is lying by definition. Gods can be opposed, fought, and killed.
- **Gods can grow into tyrannies.** A god that monopolizes its people endangers them and everything else — and the world corrects. Gods die.
- **Therefore gods are content, not rule-sources.** Faith is a relationship with a powerful being, not a subscription to cosmic truth. The divine tier is statted NPCs and campaign threats, not a metaphysical authority the rules appeal to.

## Why monsters exist

- **Creatures can change their own phenotype during their lives.** Every successful mutation in a bloodline leaves a mark. Unused marks go dormant and re-emerge in descendants when needed, in new forms.
- **Mutation is an answer to pressure, not random novelty.** Its form depends on the resources and pressures experienced. Patterns recur across species because they are answers to recurring problems.
- **The common patterns** (the naturalist's vocabulary): *Reproducer* — baseline; variability through numbers in stable times. *Scavenger* — the scarcity specialist; endures. *Tyrant* — nature's answer to overpopulation; maximum performance, often cannibal; the classic "monster." *Sage* — teacher and coordinator of long-lived kinds. *Hero* — the immune system; purges tyrants and corrupt sages; burns bright, dies young. *Scourge* — the war engine; appears when a species has a clear enemy. Patterns can mix, and mixtures make the memorable ones.
- **A monster is a diagnosis, not a species.** Monsters are what populations under pressure produce. A learned observer reads the pressures and predicts the pattern.
- **Why they stay rare.** Special variants do not breed true: reproduction merely biases offspring toward the pattern, and most descendants revert to baseline — so monsters never become species. The patterns are regulators and self-limit: tyrants cull their own and invite heroes, scourges fade with the enemy, scavengers revert when resources return. In stable times the reproducer dominates and specials dilute. To some species variants are rare accidents; to others, mutation is a life phase, like larva to butterfly.
- **Why they never quite go extinct.** The pattern is not carried by a separate population that can be wiped out. It sleeps in the whole bloodline as dormant marks. Kill every visible specimen and you have killed carriers, not the pattern. Species can die; patterns wait.

---

*The whole contract in one line: every gain in order is paid for in disorder, and whatever grows past its support collapses. This is as true of spells and monsters as of empires and gods.*
